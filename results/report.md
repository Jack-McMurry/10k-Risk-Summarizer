# 10-K Risk Comparison: 20 companies

Processed 269,455 words of risk disclosures in 13 seconds.

## Analysis

**Sector Comparison: Top 10-K Risks for 20 Companies**

**1. Risks shared across nearly all companies**

- **Cybersecurity** is the universal risk. All 20 companies list it, and 13 rate it severity 4 or 5. Banks, asset managers, and insurers all cite data theft, ransomware, and vendor breaches.
- **Regulatory and compliance burden** appears in almost every filing, usually framed as rising costs and possible fines.
- **Operational and third-party failures** (outages, errors, vendor dependence) are nearly universal.
- **Macro and market sensitivity** appears in every sector, but in different forms: fee income for asset managers, rates and credit for banks, and investment values for insurers.

**2. How the sectors differ**

- **Asset Management & Markets** risk is driven by revenue sensitivity. Fees track asset levels and trading volumes, so market downturns hit income directly. Client attrition, fee compression, and passive-fund competition are central. Schwab is the exception, with a banking-like balance sheet.
- **Banking** risk centers on the balance sheet: credit losses, net interest margin, deposit stability, and capital rules. Commercial real estate and loan reserve adequacy are recurring concerns.
- **Insurance** risk is dominated by underwriting: reserve adequacy, catastrophe losses, and inflation or social inflation in claims. Reinsurer credit and rating downgrades are also key. Insurers rarely mention deposits or liquidity runs.

**3. Standouts within each sector**

*Asset Management & Markets*
- **Schwab** has the highest concentration of severe risks: four severity-5 ratings covering rate sensitivity, sweep-cash outflows, liquidity, and cyber.
- **T. Rowe Price** has three severity-5 risks, all tied to fee revenue: market declines, performance-driven outflows, and fee cuts toward passive funds.
- **CME** stands out for trading-volume dependence and a severity-5 operational event (the November 2025 outage).
- **BlackRock** has a single severity-5 risk but a large securities-lending indemnity (about $353 billion) that is unusual in scale.

*Banking*
- **JPM, KEY, HBAN, TFC, FITB, and NTRS** each carry two severity-5 risks. KEY (72% large commercial loans) and HBAN (60% commercial, reserves against $2.7B) show the most concentrated credit exposure.
- **State Street** has no severity-5 ratings but seven severity-4 risks, the broadest moderate exposure in the group.
- **PNC** is the mildest profile, with no severity-5 risks and mostly 3s and 4s.

*Insurance*
- **Progressive** has four severity-5 risks: pricing accuracy, reserves, climate catastrophes, and cyber.
- **Cincinnati Financial** has three: reserves, inflation, and catastrophes, with about 40% of investments in equities.
- **AFG** has the fewest severe ratings (one), though it carries large reinsurance recoverables ($5.5B).

**4. Three most exposed companies overall**

1. **Charles Schwab (SCHW):** Four severity-5 risks, and they interact. Rate moves can cut net interest income while sweep-cash outflows force costlier funding, and stress could then limit access to liquidity. This is the most tightly linked risk cluster in the set.
2. **Progressive (PGR):** Four severity-5 risks spanning pricing, reserves, catastrophes, and cyber. Errors in any one of these can compound, and its exposure to weather and climate losses is especially concentrated.
3. **T. Rowe Price (TROW):** Three severity-5 risks, all tied to fee revenue. Market declines, performance-driven redemptions, and fee cuts can reinforce one another, and most revenue comes from contracts that clients can terminate on short notice.

*Caveat:* Severity scores are self-reported in each summary and are not comparable across sectors. The counts reflect only the risks listed, not the full filings.
